Shrink photos for email – on iPhone and Mac

Attachment too big? Here's how to shrink photos for email, forms and job applications on iPhone and Mac without making them blurry.

A single iPhone photo is often 3 to 5 MB, and in HEIC format sometimes has more pixels than any screen will ever show. Send ten of them by email and you quickly hit the limit of many providers – often around 20 to 25 MB per message. Online forms, job portals and government uploads often allow only a few megabytes per file, too.

What a photo really needs

  • For viewing on screen: 1280 to 1920 pixels on the long side is plenty.
  • Format: JPEG opens everywhere – including Windows PCs and older programs that can't handle HEIC.
  • Quality: 70 to 80 % is barely visible to the eye but saves a lot of space.

That often turns a 4 MB photo into a file of just a few hundred kilobytes.

How to do it with Resize Everything

  1. Tap “Choose from Photos” or “Choose from Files” (on Mac: drag the image into the window).
  2. Crop the image if you like – freely or to a fixed ratio such as 1:1 or 4:3.
  3. Tap “E-Mail”: JPEG, 1280 pixels, 70 % quality.
  4. The new size is shown at the bottom before you export.
  5. Tap “Export”, then share it, save it to Photos or attach it to your email.

If you like, the app removes metadata (location, camera details) when exporting – handy when photos go to strangers.

Without an extra app

The Mail app on iPhone sometimes asks for an image size (“Small”, “Medium”, “Large”) when you send several photos. But that only works there – for forms, messengers or websites you need the smaller file itself.
