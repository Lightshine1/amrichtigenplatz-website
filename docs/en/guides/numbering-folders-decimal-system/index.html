Numbering folders the decimal way – a filing plan for your Mac and office

Order with a system: how decimal filing (filing plans, decimal classification, Johnny.Decimal) works, why ten slots per level are enough, and how to jump into any folder on a Mac by typing digits.

“Where did I put that again?” – this question costs time in every office, every day. A proven remedy is more than a hundred years old: folders are not just named but numbered using a decimal system. In German offices it is known as the Aktenplan (filing plan), libraries know it as decimal classification, and more recently it has become popular as Johnny.Decimal.

The principle: ten slots per level

Each level holds at most ten folders, numbered 0 to 9. Each of these can in turn hold up to ten subfolders, and so on. The number goes at the front of the name:

0 Inbox
1 Company
   1 Accounting
   5 Projects
      1 Current projects
      6 Quotes
2 Personal
…
9 Archive

The “Quotes” folder therefore has the address 1-5-6: Company → Projects → Quotes. Every sequence of digits leads to exactly one folder.

Why ten?

  • Easy to take in: you can see ten entries at a glance, without scrolling.
  • Keeps you tidy: if you need an eleventh slot, you have to combine things or go one level deeper – which keeps the structure clean.
  • Goes a long way: three levels already give you 1,000 places, four levels 10,000.
  • Easy to remember: after a short while you know the important numbers by heart – like phone numbers.
  • Stable order: you decide the order, not the alphabet. Important things stay at the top, even if their name starts with “Z”.

Proven rules

  • 0 for inbox and general items: 0 works well for everything that still needs filing, or for things that apply across areas.
  • 9 for the archive: finished work moves to the back but stays within reach.
  • Don't reuse numbers: when you retire an area, leave its number free for a while, so a familiar sequence of digits doesn't suddenly lead somewhere else.
  • Not too deep: three or four levels are almost always enough. Below that, folders can have ordinary names without numbers, such as one folder per client or year.
  • One plan for everything: ideally the same plan covers paper files, computer files and email folders.

Where the system comes from

In 1876 the American Melvil Dewey arranged library collections into ten main classes with decimal subdivisions. This later gave rise to the Universal Decimal Classification, which many libraries still use today. Public authorities and companies adopted the principle for their filing plans. Johnny.Decimal is a modern variant for computers: areas such as 10–19, categories such as 11 within them, and entries such as 11.01. Which notation you choose is a matter of taste – what matters is a fixed number for every place.

On the Mac: digits instead of the mouse

Even with numbers, Finder makes you click through folder after folder. It's quicker with Ordner-Menü, our free menu bar app (in German):

  1. Press the shortcut (default: ⌃ Space).
  2. Type the digits, for example 1, 5, 6 – each digit goes one level deeper.
  3. Press Return: the folder opens in Finder.

Filing works the same way: drag files onto the menu bar icon, type the digits, press Return.
